Winamp to Return in 2027 with Deezer-Powered Streaming

The iconic music player is pivoting to a new subscription model that integrates local files, cloud libraries, and a full streaming catalog.

Radwan AbdulhadiFounder and editor, RDWAN Tech
Published: September 20, 2026
Updated: September 20, 2026 · 3 min read

Key points

  • Winamp will launch a new subscription-based player in 2027 featuring Deezer-backed infrastructure.
  • The platform will support local files, podcasts, Internet radio, and cloud libraries alongside the streaming service.
  • Deezer will provide the white-label licensing and streaming technology to overcome previous integration hurdles.
  • The classic desktop version will remain operational alongside the new, modernized platform.

A Modern Evolution for a Legacy Brand

Winamp, historically recognized as the definitive desktop MP3 player of the pre-streaming era, is [making a comeback next year] in a significantly evolved form. After years of iterative updates and changes in ownership, the brand is positioning itself for a major market return in 2027. Unlike its previous iterations, the upcoming service will shift focus toward a premium subscription model designed to compete within the crowded modern streaming market.

The platform is moving away from its roots as a standalone desktop player for local media. While the original application remains in use by more than 40 million active users globally, the new project aims to offer a holistic digital listening experience. According to the company, this reborn version will sit alongside the legacy desktop client, ensuring that long-term users are not forced to abandon their existing workflows while testing the new features.

The Strategic Partnership with Deezer

The core of this relaunch is a strategic white-label agreement with Deezer. Under this arrangement, Deezer will provide the technical infrastructure and the licensed music catalog required to stream professional content. Winamp Group, the parent entity, will maintain ownership of the branding, the core product interface, and the direct relationships with subscribers.

This partnership addresses the technical and legal challenges that stalled previous attempts to modernize the software, such as the [streaming-friendly revival] that failed to materialize in 2019. By outsourcing the complex licensing and streaming operations to Deezer, Winamp avoids the need to negotiate individual rights with labels, allowing the development team to focus on the software interface and user discovery tools.

Shifting Business Dynamics

The deal with Winamp represents a broader shift for Deezer, which has faced recent downward pressure on its subscription numbers. In the first half of 2026, the company reported a decline in new subscriptions from partner channels, dropping to 3.1 million. Revenue from its partner segment also saw a 7.6 percent decline compared to the previous year. To address these metrics, Deezer has made white-label streaming partnerships—similar to agreements signed with EDF and Telenor—a primary strategic goal.

For Winamp, the timing is critical. The brand’s trajectory has been turbulent, marked by a failed acquisition by AOL, several ownership changes, and the release of source code on GitHub in 2024 that was later withdrawn due to licensing controversies. By aligning with Deezer, Winamp hopes to stabilize its business model and finally provide the comprehensive experience promised to fans of [your parents' favorite MP3 software] for nearly a decade.

Anticipating the 2027 Launch

While details on pricing and supported device platforms remain undisclosed, Winamp has stated that its subscription tier will offer a listening experience that is distinct from existing market leaders. The development team has committed to providing a highly customizable interface, social integration features, and advanced tools for users to organize both local music collections and cloud-stored libraries. As the 2027 target date approaches, more previews and product demonstrations are expected to surface.

Industry observers are keeping a close watch on the venture. Whether Winamp can successfully transition from a nostalgic desktop utility to a viable, modern streaming platform remains to be seen. However, the partnership with established infrastructure from [Engadget] coverage suggests that this attempt is backed by a more robust technological framework than previous, failed reboot efforts.
