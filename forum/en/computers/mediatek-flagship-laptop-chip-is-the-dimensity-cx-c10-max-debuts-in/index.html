MediaTek Debuts Dimensity CX C10 Max for Lenovo Googlebook

The chipmaker is expanding the Googlebook ecosystem with its new 3nm Compute Experiences silicon, targeting high-performance AI tasks and fanless portability.

Radwan AbdulhadiFounder and editor, Mikhbar
Published: September 22, 2026
Updated: September 22, 2026 · 4 min read

Key points

  • MediaTek unveils the Dimensity CX C10 Max as its flagship chip for laptops.
  • The chip features a 3nm process, an octa-core 'All Big Core' design, and an 11-core GPU.
  • It delivers 55 TOPS of AI performance to support intelligent tasks and multitasking.
  • Lenovo will feature the chip in an upcoming Googlebook product, likely a tablet device.

MediaTek Expands the Compute Experiences Lineup

Following years of market momentum from competitors like Qualcomm, MediaTek is officially entering the premium laptop space with its new CX line of "Compute Experiences" chips. The cornerstone of this initiative is the newly announced Dimensity CX C10 Max, a flagship processor engineered to provide substantial performance gains for modern mobile computing. As reported by [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2026/09/21/mediatek-googlebook-dimensity-cx-c10-max/), this launch marks a significant strategic pivot for the company as it seeks to support the burgeoning [Googlebook](https://9to5google.com/guides/googlebook/) platform with high-end, efficient silicon.

While the initial wave of hardware in this category launched with offerings from Intel and Qualcomm, [MediaTek](https://9to5google.com/guides/mediatek/) has confirmed it will be a major participant in the platform's future. The C10 Max represents the company's commitment to delivering flagship performance without the traditional constraints of fan-based cooling or limited battery life, which often plague high-performance portable computing.

Technical Specifications and Architectural Design

At the heart of the Dimensity CX C10 Max is a sophisticated 3nm manufacturing process built on TSMC’s advanced node. The chip utilizes an "All Big Core" architecture designed to maximize throughput for demanding applications. The CPU configuration includes one Arm Cortex-X925 core for peak performance, three Arm Cortex-X4 cores, and four Arm Cortex-A720 cores. This octa-core arrangement is paired with an 11-core GPU, which, according to the company, provides the necessary graphical overhead for entertainment and high-resolution displays.

One of the standout features of this new chipset is its dedicated 8th-generation NPU. This neural processing unit is specifically designed to handle AI-driven workloads, offering up to 55 TOPs (trillion operations per second) of performance. This level of AI-first capability is intended to enhance intelligent assistance, background multitasking, and local productivity tasks. MediaTek emphasizes that this power-efficient architecture allows manufacturers to create devices that are significantly thinner and lighter than traditional laptops, while still maintaining all-day battery life.

Integration into the Lenovo Googlebook Ecosystem

The primary hardware partner for this new silicon will be [Lenovo](https://9to5google.com/guides/lenovo/). While the tech giant already debuted a 15-inch Googlebook powered by Intel, the Dimensity CX C10 Max is slated for a different upcoming product. Industry observers and internal reporting suggest that this chip is likely intended for a device previously referenced in leaks as [it’s for Lenovo’s leaked tablet](https://9to5google.com/2026/07/31/googlebook-lenovo-leak/), which runs on the specialized Googlebook OS.

This tablet integration highlights the platform's versatility. By leveraging the C10 Max's fanless design, the manufacturer can maintain a slim form factor ideal for a tablet that requires laptop-grade performance. As the ecosystem matures, the inclusion of MediaTek as a tier-one chipset provider provides consumers with more variety in terms of price points and performance tiers within the Googlebook portfolio.

Connectivity and Future-Proofing

Beyond raw compute and AI power, the Dimensity CX C10 Max is built to integrate modern connectivity standards. The chipset supports Wi-Fi 7, enabling faster wireless networking speeds and reduced latency for users in professional or home office environments. While the documentation notes that features like Bluetooth 6.0 are supported in the chipset's silicon, they are not expected to be implemented in the initial wave of Googlebook devices.

The company [explains](https://www.mediatek.com/press-room/mediatek-dimensity-cx-c10-max-offers-flagship-performance-to-a-new-era-of-ai-first-laptops) that this platform is part of a broader vision to bring "AI-first" computing to a wider range of users. By stripping away the need for bulky cooling solutions and power-hungry components, the C10 Max seeks to redefine the expectations for what a mobile workstation can be, focusing on efficiency and the seamless integration of intelligent features directly into the operating system.

Broader Market Context

The launch of the C10 Max comes at a pivotal moment as the broader Googlebook platform begins to reach consumers. With information such as the fact that [Google fully details Googlebooks: $899+, October launch, & more](https://9to5google.com/2026/09/21/googlebook-launch/), the market is currently assessing the viability of these new machines. MediaTek’s role in providing a competitive alternative to established x86 and ARM-based rivals is critical for the long-term adoption of the platform.

Industry experts are also keeping a close eye on the software experience, particularly with [Googlebook OS hands-on: It’s Android, stupid](https://9to5google.com/2026/09/21/googlebook-os-hands-on/). As the OS continues to evolve, the performance provided by chips like the C10 Max will likely dictate how well the software manages heavy multitasking and complex AI tasks. As more manufacturers adopt this new silicon, MediaTek hopes to cement its place as a key architect in the future of the laptop industry.
