Music History Podcast No Dogs in Space Returns for Season 4

The obsessive music history series returns with a renewed focus on punk rock narratives and a brand-new multi-platform distribution strategy.

Radwan AbdulhadiFounder and editor, RDWAN Tech
Published: September 21, 2026
Updated: September 21, 2026 · 3 min read

Key points

  • No Dogs in Space has returned for its fourth season, Punk 2.0, following a two-year production break.
  • Hosts Carolina Hidalgo and Marcus Parks are refocusing the show on punk rock, emphasizing its storytelling strengths.
  • The new season includes deep dives into bands like the New York Dolls and features experimental content segments.
  • For the first time in the show's history, episodes are being released as full-length videos on YouTube.

The Return of a Cult Favorite

The long-running music history podcast No Dogs in Space has officially returned to the airwaves after a hiatus lasting more than two years. The show, which is hosted by Carolina Hidalgo and Marcus Parks, announced the debut of its fourth season, subtitled Punk 2.0. Listeners can access the podcast through major platforms like Apple Podcasts, Spotify, or via the [first episode](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/no-dogs-in-space/id1495604041) which launched earlier this week.

The inaugural installments of the new season focus on the New York Dolls. The band holds a significant place in music history, and the hosts are utilizing this series to kick off their goal of getting 'back to basics.' According to reporting from [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/report/997948/no-dogs-in-space-is-back-punk-2-0-music-history-podcast), the pair decided that punk rock is the area where their research and expertise are most effective.

Navigating Production Challenges

The two-year gap between seasons was partially the result of several failed project attempts. The duo initially spent six months researching a series on the Washington D.C. punk scene, intending to feature the band [Bad Brains](https://youtu.be/gnWKhq8l_5c) as a primary focal point. However, the hosts ultimately decided against the project after encountering difficulties with the subject matter, citing the band's history of problematic views.

The production team also attempted a broader overview of the D.C. scene, which proved to be too large in scope. The investigation initially touched on foundational influences such as [Chuck Brown](https://youtu.be/9ZjeheDR3tA) and the genre of go-go. After eight months of effort, the hosts determined that they could not synthesize the vast number of stories and entities into a cohesive narrative, leading them to admit defeat on that specific project. Despite this, some of the research will be utilized for an upcoming two-part series on musician Link Wray.

Embracing Obsessive Storytelling

A defining characteristic of the podcast is the hosts' tendency to wander into historical 'sidequests' and rabbit holes. During the current season, the hosts have explored topics such as the career of Ian Hunter from the band Mott the Hoople. Hunter, known for his work on [All the Young Dudes](https://youtu.be/yNHdPPJGowY) and the song [Cleveland Rocks](https://youtu.be/1qDkZnpOiS8), serves as an example of the unique character studies that populate the show.

These deep dives are framed by the hosts as a 'superpower.' Both Parks and Hidalgo recently received autism diagnoses, which they now credit for their ability to focus intensely on niche historical details. Parks noted that their natural inclination is to live in a 'state of obsession,' a trait that directly informs the depth of the research provided to their listeners.

New Distribution Channels

In a notable shift for the series, No Dogs in Space is expanding its reach beyond audio-only platforms. For the first time, full-length video episodes are being uploaded to YouTube, allowing for a more immersive experience that complements the traditional podcast format. This change follows the show's goal of reaching a wider audience while maintaining the high standards of research and storytelling that fans have come to expect from the [No Dogs in Space](https://www.lastpodcastnetwork.com/no-dogs-in-space) brand.
