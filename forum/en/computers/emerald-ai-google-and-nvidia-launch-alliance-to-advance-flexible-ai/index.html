Emerald AI, Google and NVIDIA Form Alliance for Flexible AI Data Centers

Emerald AI, Google and NVIDIA have announced the AI Energy Management Alliance, a coalition focused on making AI data centers more responsive to grid conditions. The group aims to develop common technical and operational approaches for connecting flexible computing facilities.

Published: September 16, 2026
Updated: September 16, 2026 · 4 min read

Key points

  • The AI Energy Management Alliance will promote data centers that can dynamically manage electricity consumption.
  • Flexible facilities could shift workloads, discharge storage, use paired generation or respond to grid emergencies.
  • The alliance plans technology-neutral, performance-based requirements for reliability and interconnection.
  • Its members will include companies and organizations across the computing and power sectors.

A new approach to AI infrastructure

Emerald AI, Google and NVIDIA have launched the AI Energy Management Alliance, or AEMA, to advance data centers capable of adjusting their electricity use in response to changing grid conditions. NVIDIA describes the initiative as a coalition spanning the AI and power value chain.

The alliance is built around the idea that future AI infrastructure should not simply connect to the electricity grid as a conventional large customer. Instead, flexible data centers could operate in ways that support the grid while continuing to provide computing capacity.

NVIDIA said the objective is to accelerate the interconnection of flexible, grid-enhancing data centers, strengthen reliability and help protect energy affordability. The company also said that making more effective use of existing infrastructure could reduce environmental impacts per watt.

Power flexibility for data centers

According to NVIDIA, power has become a defining constraint on the expansion of AI infrastructure in the United States. Traditional interconnection processes were designed largely for facilities with steady, static electricity demand, rather than computing sites able to respond intelligently when the power system is under pressure.

A flexible data center can manage its electricity draw in several ways. It may shift computing workloads, discharge energy storage, use paired generation or respond to contingencies affecting the wider power system. These capabilities can allow a large electricity customer to function as a controllable resource instead of an inflexible load.

NVIDIA said this flexibility could improve the use of available grid capacity, reduce demand during periods of system stress and avoid or defer some costly infrastructure upgrades. It could also give utilities and grid operators greater confidence when connecting AI facilities on shorter timelines.

Technology-neutral requirements

AEMA plans to use technology-neutral and performance-based requirements. Rather than prescribing particular hardware or software, the alliance will focus on measurable services that a facility can deliver, including response speed, duration, predictability and behavior during an emergency.

Reliability is central to the alliance's stated principles. These include defining ride-through, curtailment and contingency-response obligations before a facility connects to the grid. In practice, those obligations would establish how a site remains connected during brief disturbances, reduces power use when required and responds to emergencies.

The alliance also intends to standardize technical requirements, performance metrics and operational data sharing. It will seek faster, risk-adjusted pathways for customers that make credible and verifiable flexibility commitments, while promoting interconnection-cost allocations that reflect actual system impacts and benefits.

Bringing the power and computing sectors together

AEMA is intended to convene organizations from across computing and electricity markets. Its participants are expected to include AI platform companies, infrastructure providers, data center operators, technology companies, power producers, utilities and regional grid operators.

The founding members will work with launch partners from across the ecosystem to develop technical and operational approaches. The group also plans to collaborate with utilities on interconnection solutions and advocate for policies that recognize demand capable of responding to grid conditions.

NVIDIA said the alliance will broaden work already being conducted by NVIDIA and Emerald AI with energy and infrastructure leaders on AI factories that can react to grid conditions in real time.

A framework for U.S. AI expansion

The alliance comes as the rules governing electricity access for AI infrastructure are still being developed. AEMA aims to create a common framework covering performance, reliability and collaboration, with models that can be deployed across the United States.

NVIDIA characterized AI factories as infrastructure for what it calls the intelligence era, with their responsible expansion depending on innovation both inside data centers and across the electricity grid. Under the alliance's approach, power-flexible design could allow these facilities to contribute to grid operations while converting energy and data into AI output.

The initiative's success will depend on the development of shared technical requirements, reliable performance data and workable arrangements among developers, utilities and grid operators. Its stated goal is to help connect AI infrastructure more quickly while preserving the reliability of the energy systems that support it.
