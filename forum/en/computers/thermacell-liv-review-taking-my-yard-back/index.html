Thermacell Releases LIV 2.0 Smart Mosquito Repellent System

The updated mosquito-abatement technology improves upon its predecessor with enhanced hardware, a faster startup time, and a more intuitive mobile application.

Radwan AbdulhadiFounder and editor, RDWAN Tech
Published: September 21, 2026
Updated: September 21, 2026 · 3 min read

Key points

  • The LIV 2.0 system features redesigned repellers that improve chemical dispersion via a new top-venting hatch.
  • A single control hub can now support up to 10 repellers, doubling the capacity of the previous generation.
  • The system utilizes metofluthrin, which is now marketed as effective against both mosquitoes and midges.
  • Installation is now handled through professional services rather than the previous DIY approach.

An Evolution in Outdoor Pest Control

Thermacell has officially introduced the LIV 2.0, an updated iteration of its smart mosquito-repellent system. This technology is engineered to create a protective barrier around specific outdoor areas, such as swimming pools or patios. As detailed in the recent Thermacell LIV 2.0 Review: Taking My Yard Back, the system operates as a daisy-chained network of hardware that releases a vaporized chemical to deter insects from entering a designated space.

Hardware and Design Enhancements

The LIV 2.0 system introduces several physical refinements compared to the original, which was previously analyzed in a report where the author wrote about it extensively for WIRED. The control hubs now offer increased versatility, allowing for two cable terminal posts rather than one, which enables the daisy chain to branch in different directions. Furthermore, the capacity of a single hub has expanded from six to ten repellers, accommodating larger outdoor layouts.

The repellers themselves have undergone a functional redesign. While the cylindrical form remains, the units now feature a motorized top hatch that opens during operation. This mechanism allows the metofluthrin vapor to rise vertically, which is intended to improve coverage. Additionally, the LED status rings have been made more visible to ensure users can clearly identify the system's power state.

Software and Chemical Efficacy

The system continues to utilize 5.5 percent metofluthrin, a chemical that insects avoid. Recent updates to EPA guidance have allowed Thermacell to market the repellent as effective against midges and no-see-ums in addition to mosquitoes. While the chemical composition remains consistent, the improved venting design helps the system reach operational effectiveness in approximately 15 minutes, a noticeable improvement over the previous generation's 30-minute warmup time.

A new mobile application has been developed to manage these settings, offering improved scheduling and timer functionality. Users can also customize the LED light colors on the repellers through this interface. While the app is noted to experience slight latency when reflecting status changes, it provides a centralized control point for the entire network.

Availability and Professional Installation

Thermacell has shifted the acquisition model for the LIV 2.0. The system is no longer marketed as a DIY installation project; prospective users are now directed to a "find a pro" service. This professional installation requirement accompanies a starting price point of $1,746 at Thermacell for a basic configuration consisting of one hub and three repellers. Ongoing maintenance costs remain, with consumable 100-hour metofluthrin cartridges priced at $250 for a six-pack.

For those interested in the broader context of smart home pest management, the manufacturer suggests that while the upgrade provides incremental benefits, it represents a specialized solution for outdoor protection. As the company continues to refine its ecosystem, potential customers are encouraged to learn more about the evolving landscape of smart home integration.
