AMD Surpasses $1 Trillion Market Cap Milestone

After a period of rapid stock growth, AMD has reached a historic $1 trillion market capitalization. The semiconductor firm now joins an exclusive group of global companies, including Nvidia, Broadcom, and SK hynix.

Radwan AbdulhadiFounder and editor, Mikhbar
Published: September 22, 2026
Updated: September 22, 2026 · 3 min read

Key points

  • AMD shares hit a record valuation, crossing the $1 trillion mark for the first time.
  • The company's stock experienced a 21% jump over a five-day period.
  • AMD successfully reached the milestone ahead of industry rival Intel.
  • Nvidia continues to lead the sector with a market capitalization of approximately $5.4 trillion.

A Historic Market Milestone

AMD has officially crossed the $1 trillion market cap threshold, marking a significant milestone in the company’s history. The firm joins a highly exclusive group of fewer than two dozen companies worldwide to reach this valuation. The news of the achievement was shared by Saša Marinković, AMD's senior marketing director, who celebrated the event on X. Detailed coverage of this market movement can be found at [Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/amd-beats-intel-to-the-trillion-dollar-club-as-stock-price-soars-firm-joins-nvidia-broadcom-and-sk-hynix-for-companies-worth-over-usd1-trillion).

This valuation peak follows a period of intense activity for the company's shares. At the close of trading on Friday, the stock was priced at roughly $557. Following the market opening on Monday, shares climbed to $607, eventually driving the company’s total market value over the $1 trillion mark. While the price has since shown some fluctuation due to typical market volatility surrounding major milestones, the growth represents a substantial rally. Further details are available from Terms & Conditions in the original source material.

Market Growth and Trajectory

The surge in AMD's market cap was rapid, with approximately 21% of its 29% monthly stock growth occurring within the last five days of the reporting period. On Monday alone, the company saw its share price jump by about 9% during the first few hours of trading. Investors have been closely watching the semiconductor sector, and this momentum follows a positive performance in the company's second-quarter earnings, where it outperformed revenue expectations.

Market capitalization serves as a reflection of Wall Street's confidence in a company's future value, calculated by multiplying outstanding shares by the current share price. While not a direct measure of revenue, the valuation highlights the competitive positioning of firms like AMD, which now sits alongside tech giants such as Microsoft, Apple, and [Amazon](https://www.tomshardware.com/tag/amazon) in the trillion-dollar category.

Competitive Landscape: AMD vs. Intel

A notable aspect of this financial achievement is that AMD has reached the trillion-dollar threshold while its longtime competitor, Intel, remains behind. Intel currently holds a market capitalization of approximately $648 billion. Interestingly, Intel has also seen significant market activity, with its share price rising by 37% over the past month. Intel's performance remains part of a broader trend of growth within the semiconductor industry, despite AMD being the first of the two to reach the trillion-dollar status.

While AMD has made notable gains, it continues to face a significant gap with industry leader Nvidia. Currently, Nvidia commands a market capitalization of approximately $5.4 trillion, maintaining a dominant lead in the AI accelerator market. Since Nvidia first hit the $1 trillion milestone in 2023, it has grown to become one of the most valuable companies in the world, setting a high bar for its competitors.

Strategic Positioning in AI

AMD's recent financial success is partially attributed to its strategic pivot toward the data center market. While Nvidia remains the primary force in AI acceleration, AMD has successfully integrated its CPU products into agentic AI data centers. This role as a critical component supplier for modern computing infrastructure has become a focal point of the company's recent growth, helping to drive investor interest and the subsequent rise in share prices.

The company’s ability to sustain this value will likely depend on its continued execution in the server and workstation processor sectors. As market conditions evolve, the semiconductor industry continues to see high interest from investors looking to capitalize on the expansion of artificial intelligence capabilities.
