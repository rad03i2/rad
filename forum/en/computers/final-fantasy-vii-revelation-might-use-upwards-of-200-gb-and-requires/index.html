Final Fantasy VII Revelation May Require 200GB Install

The concluding entry in the Final Fantasy VII remake series is poised to become a massive storage hog, likely setting a new record for base installation size.

Radwan AbdulhadiFounder and editor, RDWAN Tech
Published: September 20, 2026
Updated: September 20, 2026 · 3 min read

Key points

  • Final Fantasy VII Revelation is estimated to exceed 200 GB in total storage requirements.
  • The physical edition will require a significant additional data download beyond the content on the UHD Blu-ray disc.
  • Director Naoki Hamaguchi cited the game's scope and visual fidelity as reasons for the large file size.
  • Two additional character backstory DLCs for Vincent and Sephiroth are planned, potentially increasing the total footprint to 220 GB.
  • The title is scheduled for release on April 8, 2027.

A Massive Footprint for a Final Chapter

As AAA game development continues to push the boundaries of visual fidelity and content density, storage management has become a critical concern for PC and console gamers alike. With high-performance NVMe SSD prices fluctuating, the upcoming release of Final Fantasy VII Revelation is drawing attention for its unprecedented size. According to recent reports found on <a href="https://www.tomshardware.com/video-games/final-fantasy-vii-revelation-might-use-upwards-of-200-gb-and-requires-an-additional-download-latest-entry-in-the-series-of-remakes-may-set-a-new-record-for-the-size-of-a-base-install">Tom's Hardware</a>, the game is expected to occupy upwards of 200 GB, potentially setting a new record for a base game installation.

The details emerged following an interview at the Tokyo Game Show between the series director, Naoki Hamaguchi, and the outlet Video Games Chronicle. During the discussion, Hamaguchi addressed the logistical challenges of distributing such a large title, confirming that <a href="https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/the-physical-edition-of-final-fantasy-7-revelation-needs-to-download-extra-game-data-on-all-formats/">the one UHD Blu-ray disc</a> included with the physical version will provide roughly 93.1 GB of data, necessitating a substantial additional download for the remaining assets.

Technical Trade-offs and Development Scope

When asked if the studio considered splitting the game across multiple physical discs to mitigate the download burden, Hamaguchi explained that such a decision would have required a compromise regarding the project's overall scope or its intended visual fidelity. He emphasized that the development team opted for a digital-heavy distribution model to ensure the best possible experience for players.

Furthermore, the director highlighted the advantages of digital content delivery, noting that it allows the team to implement patches and final optimizations right up until the game's launch. This stands in contrast to the traditional disc manufacturing cycle, which typically requires approximately three months of lead time to finalize and produce.

DLC Expansions and Final Tally

The storage requirement is further compounded by planned post-launch content. Hamaguchi revealed that two character-focused backstory DLCs are currently in development for the title. These expansions will explore the histories of Vincent and the fan-favorite ōdachi-wielder, Sephiroth. To <a href="https://www.videogameschronicle.com/features/interviews/ff7-revelation-director-hamaguchi-discusses-the-games-200gb-size-dlc-plans-and-a-new-tifa-detail/">make way for</a> these additional stories, gamers may need to set aside even more space, with total estimates for a fully loaded install reaching between 215 GB and 220 GB.

Preparation for the April 2027 Launch

With the official release date locked for April 8, 2027, players have a significant window of time to upgrade their storage solutions. While console owners on the PlayStation 5 platform can leverage compatible, off-the-shelf NVMe drives to expand their capacity by up to 8 TB, Xbox users face a more restrictive ecosystem requiring proprietary, higher-cost expansion modules that generally cap out at 4 TB.

By prioritizing this upcoming release, gamers are likely to continue the trend of monitoring their drive usage closely. As titles continue to grow in complexity, the industry appears to be moving into an era where 200 GB installations may become the new standard for flagship gaming experiences.
