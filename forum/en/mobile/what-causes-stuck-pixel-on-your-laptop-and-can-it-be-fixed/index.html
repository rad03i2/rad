Stuck Pixels on Laptops: Causes and Potential Fixes

A single malfunctioning pixel does not necessarily mean your laptop is ruined. By understanding the causes behind these persistent spots, users can attempt several recovery techniques to restore their display.

Radwan AbdulhadiFounder and editor, RDWAN Tech
Published: September 21, 2026
Updated: September 21, 2026 · 4 min read

Key points

  • Stuck pixels are caused by transistors receiving too much power, resulting in a pixel that remains fixed on a single color.
  • Common triggers include physical damage, power surges, temperature extremes, or natural hardware aging.
  • Software-based solutions that cycle colors are the safest methods for attempting a repair.
  • Physical techniques involving pressure or heat require extreme caution to avoid further damage to the display panel.
  • Warranty coverage for display issues often depends on the number of malfunctioning pixels present.

Identifying the Problem

Finding a strange, multicolored dot on your display can be alarming, but it is important to distinguish a stuck pixel from a dead one or cases of screen burn-in. Unlike a dead pixel, which shows no light, a stuck pixel is characterized by light emanating from behind it, appearing as red, green, blue, or a combination of those hues. According to [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2257814/laptop-stuck-pixel-causes-fixes/), this indicates that the pixel still has power flowing through it, meaning there is potential for a fix.

Stuck pixels occur because the transistor controlling the pixel has become stuck in the 'on' position, preventing the pixel from changing color as intended. This is fundamentally different from [screen burn-in](https://www.engadget.com/2246417/tvs-at-risk-of-burn-in/), which is a more severe and permanent degradation of a display. While a single stuck pixel is frustrating, it does not mean your entire screen is destined for failure, as these issues typically do not spread like an infection.

Common Causes of Stuck Pixels

Even when purchasing the [best laptops](https://www.engadget.com/computing/laptops/best-laptops-120008636.html) currently on the market, users are not entirely immune to this issue. Physical damage is a frequent culprit; dropping a device can damage internal transistors, while excessive pressure on the screen can disturb the liquid crystals in an LCD panel. Furthermore, exposure to extreme temperatures or power surges can compromise internal components, leading to display artifacts.

Aging equipment also plays a significant role in display malfunctions. As a device gets older, internal components are more likely to fail. Additionally, broader manufacturing defects—such as faulty transistors or improper sealing—can result in pixel issues. If these defects are present, you might notice multiple pixels failing over time rather than just a single point.

Software Solutions for Restoration

The safest way to attempt a repair is through software designed to stimulate the pixel's transistor. Tools like [JScreenFix](https://www.jscreenfix.com/) provide a web-based solution that cycles colors rapidly to help 'unstick' the pixel. Another popular option is [PixelHealer](https://www.aurelitec.com/pixelhealer/), a dedicated program for Windows users. These tools work by forcing the pixel to cycle through various colors, which can reset the transistor.

Users should exercise caution when using these software tools, especially if they or anyone nearby suffers from photosensitive epilepsy. Before diving into software, a simple yet effective first step is to turn off your device for a full day, which can sometimes provide enough time for the pixel to reset on its own.

Manual and Physical Repair Techniques

If software proves ineffective, some users attempt to fix the pixel using physical pressure or heat. To use the pressure method, wrap a soft, blunt object like a stylus or pen cap with a microfiber cloth. With the screen powered off, apply light pressure directly to the affected area. Power the device on while maintaining that pressure to see if the pixel resets. This process can be repeated as necessary.

The heat method involves using a warmed cloth—never hot—placed against the area of the stuck pixel for several minutes. While both physical methods can be effective, they carry the risk of causing further harm to the display panel or internal components. Always proceed with extreme caution and allow for breaks between sessions to ensure the device does not overheat.

Warranty and Professional Considerations

Before attempting any physical repairs, check your device's warranty status. Manufacturers vary in their policies regarding display malfunctions. A single stuck pixel often does not meet the threshold for a free replacement or repair, though a cluster of pixels might. If your screen has a recurring issue that suggests a manufacturing defect, contacting the manufacturer is the most prudent course of action to avoid voiding your warranty through DIY attempts.
