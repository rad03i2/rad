Boston Dynamics Opens Metaplant Application Center for Atlas

The new facility, located at Hyundai’s Metaplant America, serves as a specialized testing ground to integrate humanoid robots into high-volume production environments.

Radwan AbdulhadiFounder and editor, RDWAN Tech
Published: September 21, 2026
Updated: September 21, 2026 · 3 min read

Key points

  • Boston Dynamics launched the Robotics Metaplant Application Center (RMAC) in Georgia to train Atlas humanoids.
  • The facility acts as a 'Robot Behavior Factory,' utilizing real automotive parts and workcells to generate training data.
  • Hyundai plans to deploy 25,000 Atlas units across its global manufacturing network over the next several years.
  • Operations at the site are scheduled to expand tenfold by next year to support global deployment capabilities.

Advancing General-Purpose Automation

Boston Dynamics has officially opened its Robotics Metaplant Application Center, or RMAC, situated at the Hyundai Motor Group Metaplant America in Georgia. This new facility functions as both a testbed and a dedicated training center, marking a significant step in the company's efforts to integrate its Atlas

robots directly into parent Hyundai’s active

manufacturing workflows. According to Zack Jackowski, chief product and technology officer at the

, the initiative is central to a broader strategy aimed at optimizing the supply chain, production testing, and deployment capabilities for Atlas as the industry pushes toward general-purpose automation.

A New 'Robot Behavior Factory'

Unlike previous trials, which were limited in scope, the RMAC is designed as a permanent facility focused on iterative data collection and real-world application testing. Jackowski, whose insights were featured by

, describes the RMAC as a 'Robot Behavior Factory.' By utilizing actual car parts, fixtures, and workcells alongside skilled human pilots, the facility produces high-quality data used to train robot behavior policies. This approach helps developers understand the operational realities of deploying technology in a live, high-speed manufacturing environment.

Mastering Dexterous Manipulation

Training at the center focuses on the complex requirements of modern car production. The robots are learning to manage the logistics and sequencing of parts, a task that demands high levels of dexterity. Because automotive components vary in weight, fragility, and surface complexity, Atlas must master bimanual manipulation, such as safely extracting items from shipping packaging or placing components into high-density cubbies.

Development teams are leveraging multiple data streams to refine these skills, including

and reinforcement learning within a digital environment. These techniques ensure that the robots can handle the nuance of interacting with fragile elements without compromising the efficiency of the production line.

Expansion and Future Outlook

The RMAC project is a cornerstone of the robotics and AI strategy Hyundai announced earlier this year. The automotive group plans to deploy 25,000 Atlas units globally across its plants over the coming years. To support this growth, Boston Dynamics is already planning a major expansion of the Georgia site; by next year, operations are expected to move into a new building that will increase the facility's footprint by approximately ten times.

While the current focus remains on automotive assembly, the company expects to begin exploring use cases in other industrial sectors by next year. Future goals include expanding capabilities into component assembly by 2030, with Atlas eventually taking on tasks involving heavy lifting and highly repetitive motions to increase workplace safety and overall productivity.
