Hirebotics Adds Line Tracking and Linear Rail to Cobots

Hirebotics has expanded the operational range and flexibility of its collaborative robots by introducing new line tracking technology and modular linear rail systems. These updates are designed to help manufacturers automate complex tasks without requiring specialized robotics programming.

Radwan AbdulhadiFounder and editor, RDWAN Tech
Published: September 20, 2026
Updated: September 20, 2026 · 3 min read

Key points

  • New line tracking allows cobots to paint moving parts on conveyor systems automatically.
  • Modular linear rails extend the reach of cobots for large-scale structural assemblies.
  • The new features integrate directly into the existing, no-code Beacon platform.
  • Automated rail movement is planned for release by the end of 2026.

Expanding Automation Capabilities

At IMTS 2026, Hirebotics unveiled new hardware extensions for its collaborative robots, aimed at allowing Manufacturers to tackle larger and more complex projects. The updates include advanced line tracking and modular linear rails, designed to give users the ability to handle moving parts and oversized workpieces without necessitating major changes to their existing production workflows.

CEO Matthew Bush stated that these developments address two common industry pain points: parts that are too large for a fixed work envelope and parts that cannot remain stationary during processing. By integrating these capabilities, the company aims to simplify the deployment of a cobot for facilities that do not have dedicated robotics or engineering teams.

Line Tracking for Dynamic Production

The new line tracking feature is specifically engineered for the Cobot Painter, enabling it to follow parts as they move along a production line. By using a sensor to detect an incoming part, the robot automatically begins its programmed path, maintaining alignment even if the conveyor speed fluctuates.

Unlike traditional systems that often require complex integration, Hirebotics built this functionality into its cloud-based Beacon platform. This allows for a no-code experience where users can teach the robot the direction of the conveyor and rely on the software to provide the necessary responsiveness for real-time, motion-based painting.

Extended Reach with Linear Rails

To address the challenge of reaching large or awkward structures, Hirebotics has introduced a modular linear rail system available in 5-foot increments or custom lengths. While a standard cobot typically operates within a 68.9-inch reach, this rail allows the robot to traverse the length of large weldments or structural assemblies.

The rails are compatible across the company's various applications, including welding and plasma cutting. This versatility is particularly beneficial for industries involved in heavy fabrication, such as shipyards, construction equipment manufacturing, and the production of tanks or large vessels, where repositioning a massive part is often time-consuming and inefficient.

Future Roadmap and Platform Integration

Currently, the linear rail system supports manual positioning, with the Beacon platform guiding operators through required changes. Hirebotics has outlined a clear development path, with plans to introduce fully automated movement by the end of 2026. This future iteration will allow the system to function as a seventh axis, completely controlled by the software.

By centralizing these advanced features within its existing ecosystem, Hirebotics continues to push its goal of making automation accessible to more manufacturers. The company emphasizes that its primary mission remains building a common platform that reduces the learning curve for teams tasked with operating complex robotic equipment.
