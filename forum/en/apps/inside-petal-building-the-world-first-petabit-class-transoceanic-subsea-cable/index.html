Meta Unveils Petal, World’s First Petabit-Class Subsea Cable

Meta’s latest subsea innovation, scheduled to enter service in 2029, will utilize groundbreaking multi-core fiber technology to achieve an unprecedented 1 petabit per second transmission capacity.

Radwan AbdulhadiFounder and editor, Mikhbar
Published: September 21, 2026
Updated: September 21, 2026 · 3 min read

Key points

  • Petal will connect France and the United States across approximately 7,000 km of ocean floor.
  • The system is designed to deliver 1 petabit per second, doubling the capacity of current advanced transatlantic cables.
  • Engineered in partnership with NEC, Sumitomo Electric Industries, and Orange, the cable deploys multi-core fiber at scale.
  • The project utilizes 2-core fiber and specialized Fan-In/Fan-Out (FIFO) repeaters to increase data density without increasing power requirements.

Breaking the Petabit Barrier

Meta has officially announced the development of Petal, a pioneering transoceanic subsea cable system designed to reach a capacity of 1 petabit per second (Pbps). This massive infrastructure project is set to span approximately 7,000 km between the United States and France, effectively doubling the capacity of the most advanced cables currently in operation. By incorporating two cores into a single fiber strand, Petal achieves an effective capacity equivalent to 48 fiber pairs within a 24 fiber-pair system. This represents the largest generational leap in repeatered subsea system capacity to date.

Overcoming Engineering Challenges

Engineering a 2-core fiber system presented significant technical hurdles, particularly in manufacturing and signal amplification. To maintain the standard physical dimensions of the fiber, manufacturers utilized ultra-pure synthetic silica during the preform production process. Additionally, by controlling the refractive indexes of the cores against the surrounding medium, engineers were able to minimize crosstalk between the two cores to near-immeasurable levels.

The cable also requires a sophisticated approach to signal amplification. A standard 7,000 km run typically necessitates roughly 100 repeaters. For Petal, the team developed a single-body 96-amp repeater that incorporates a Fan-In/Fan-Out (FIFO) interface. This mechanism transitions 2-core fiber into two single-core fibers for the amplification process, then converts them back, allowing the system to leverage high-efficiency single-core amplification without needing to change existing power feeding equipment limits.

Strategic Partnerships and Future Outlook

The project is a result of extensive collaboration between Meta and several industry leaders. NEC Corporation is acting as the turnkey system supplier, having invested heavily in the manufacturing of petabit-class repeaters and multi-core systems. Eduardo Mateo, Chief Strategy Officer of NEC’s Submarine Network Division, described the milestone as a major achievement in global telecommunications history.

Sumitomo Electric Industries provided the specialized “2C Z-PLUS ULL Fiber,” which is central to Petal’s optical performance. Meanwhile, Orange is working alongside Meta to facilitate the landing of the cable on the French Atlantic coast and manage the subsequent terrestrial integration into the European network. With a target service date of 2029, Petal is positioned to set a new standard for transoceanic data transmission.
