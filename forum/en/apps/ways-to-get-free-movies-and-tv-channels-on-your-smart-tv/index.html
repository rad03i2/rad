How to Stream Free Movies and TV Shows on Your Smart TV

Consumers can bypass the rising costs of subscription services by utilizing a variety of legitimate, ad-supported streaming platforms available on most smart television devices.

Radwan AbdulhadiFounder and editor, RDWAN Tech
Published: September 21, 2026
Updated: September 21, 2026 · 3 min read

Key points

  • Most modern smart TVs come equipped with built-in FAST services for instant access to live and on-demand content.
  • Third-party applications like Tubi and Pluto TV provide extensive libraries of movies and classic shows.
  • Library cardholders can access ad-free content through specialized services like Hoopla and Kanopy.
  • Major platforms, including YouTube and Amazon Prime Video, maintain sections dedicated to free, ad-supported programming.

The Rise of FAST Services

In an era defined by the always-increasing prices of paid services like Disney+, many viewers are turning toward Free Ad-Supported Streaming Television (FAST) services. These platforms provide a blend of on-demand libraries and linear, channel-surfing experiences that emulate traditional cable television. Most smart TV manufacturers include native FAST applications, such as LG Channels, Samsung TV Plus, The Roku Channel, and Google TV Freeplay. For those using Vizio hardware, the interface is known as WatchFree+.

These built-in services offer a wide range of content, including news, sports highlights, cooking shows, and reality programming. While users may not find the latest Hollywood blockbusters, these services provide a robust catalog for casual viewing. Because the content is provided at no cost, users should anticipate intermittent advertising breaks during their sessions.

Dedicated Third-Party Streaming Apps

Beyond pre-installed manufacturer software, standalone applications offer more extensive options for viewers. Tubi stands out as a prominent choice, requiring no subscription for access to a vast array of movies and television series. According to the platform, the frequency of advertisements is lower than traditional broadcasting, which helps maintain the viewer experience. Tubi also organizes content into curated collections, including specific network breakdowns and original productions.

Pluto TV offers a similar experience but places a stronger emphasis on a linear, 24/7 channel format. It is designed for those who prefer the traditional cable experience, featuring dedicated channels for specific genres such as thriller, action, or science fiction. Users can also access music video channels if you miss the glory days of MTV, making it a versatile tool for background entertainment.

Leveraging Library Resources

For viewers seeking ad-free content, public libraries provide access to professional streaming services through membership cards. Platforms like Hoopla and Kanopy allow users to stream high-quality educational and entertainment content at no expense. Hoopla is particularly noted for its inclusion of British television and unique 'BingePasses,' while Kanopy caters to those interested in documentaries and international cinema. Beyond video, these library-linked services often provide access to a wide array of resources, including free ebooks.

Navigating YouTube and Prime Video

Major video platforms also host significant libraries of free, legally available content. Users can navigate to YouTube's official free streaming library by selecting the 'Movies & TV' tab to find ad-supported studio content. Additionally, the platform hosts various public domain films and content from channels like Warner Bros. Classics and Shout! Studios.

Amazon has also integrated its free content directly into the Prime Video interface. Following the discontinuation of the dedicated Freevee app in 2025, the company folded its free-to-stream movies and television shows into the primary Prime Video application. Users should be mindful when browsing to distinguish these free options from premium content that requires a subscription or a one-time purchase fee.
