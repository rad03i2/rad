OpenAI Establishes Independent Math Advisory Group

In response to rapid progress in AI-driven mathematics, including the resolution of the Navier–Stokes Millennium Prize problem, OpenAI is formalizing its engagement with the academic community.

Radwan AbdulhadiFounder and editor, Mikhbar
Published: September 21, 2026
Updated: September 21, 2026 · 3 min read

Key points

  • OpenAI formed an independent Advisory Group on Mathematics and Artificial Intelligence to bridge the gap between AI development and the mathematical community.
  • The new internal model has successfully resolved over 100 long-standing open mathematical problems.
  • The advisory group maintains full independence, with the power to issue public statements and critiques regarding OpenAI’s impact on the field.
  • Key academic figures, including Timothy Gowers and Martin Hairer, serve on the initial board hosted at the Institute for Advanced Study.

Navigating Rapid Advancements in Mathematical AI

OpenAI has officially announced the formation of an independent [Advisory Group on Mathematics and Artificial Intelligence](http://agmai.org) to help manage the implications of its recent progress in computational mathematics. The initiative follows a period of accelerated internal development that began on August 28, during which the [pace of its progress](https://openai.com/index/navier-stokes-solution/#astra-internal-model-open-math-problems) surprised even the company's internal team of mathematicians.

The company’s latest internal model has achieved significant milestones, most notably [resolving the Navier–Stokes Millennium Prize problem](https://openai.com/index/navier-stokes-solution/). Beyond this specific accomplishment, the model has reportedly solved more than 100 other long-standing, open mathematical problems across a diverse range of disciplines. This rapid output has prompted internal discussions at the [Company](https://openai.com/news/company-announcements/) regarding how best to inform the broader scientific community and adapt professional standards to this new era of AI research.

Responding to Concerns from the Mathematical Community

The integration of AI into fundamental research has not been without controversy. In a widely discussed open letter titled "[A Severe Misalignment of AI in Mathematics](https://mathandai.org/)", prominent mathematicians expressed concerns regarding the negative externalities associated with using high-stakes open problems as benchmarks for new machine learning systems.

These criticisms underscored a perceived disconnect between AI developers and the mathematical community. By establishing the new advisory group, OpenAI aims to provide a formal channel for mathematicians to voice their concerns and contribute to the ethical development of mathematical AI tools. The company stated that the group will act as a bridge to ensure that these advancements are handled with appropriate academic and professional rigor.

The Mandate and Independence of the Advisory Group

A key tenet of the new advisory body is its complete operational independence from OpenAI. The group is empowered to offer unsolicited advice, publish its findings publicly, and comment on the direct impact of OpenAI's technologies on the field of mathematics. To preserve its objective stance, members of the group are not compensated by the company and retain full authority over their internal membership composition.

While the group will advise on the review and communication of emerging results and help assess the significance of AI-led breakthroughs, its scope does have limitations. Specifically, the group will not have the authority to advise on the pace at which OpenAI conducts its internal mathematical research, focusing instead on the dissemination and responsible deployment of those findings.

Leadership and Future Coordination

The initial members of the [Advisory Group on Mathematics and Artificial Intelligence](http://agmai.org), which is being hosted at the [Institute for Advanced Study](https://www.ias.edu/nccr/projects#agmai), include Timothy Gowers of the Collège de France and Cambridge, Martin Hairer of EPFL and Imperial College London, and Nikhil Srivastava from Berkeley and the Simons Institute. These experts will help OpenAI refine its approach to utilizing AI as a tool to support, rather than replace, human-led mathematical research.

OpenAI maintains that working with this group is a primary step in a longer-term strategy. As the company continues to explore how AI can assist in broader scientific discovery, it intends for mathematicians to remain at the center of the conversation regarding the integration and eventual benefits of these powerful computational capabilities.
