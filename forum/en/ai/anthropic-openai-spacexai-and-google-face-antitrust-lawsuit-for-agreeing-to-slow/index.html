Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, and SpaceXAI Face Antitrust Lawsuit

A proposed class-action lawsuit has been filed against four major AI developers, challenging the legality of their coordinated efforts to decelerate artificial intelligence progress.

Radwan AbdulhadiFounder and editor, RDWAN Tech
Published: September 20, 2026
Updated: September 20, 2026 · 3 min read

Key points

  • Plaintiffs allege major AI labs engaged in illegal antitrust behavior by coordinating to slow development.
  • The lawsuit claims the initiative is a 'self-serving' attempt to bypass individual accountability.
  • Legal counsel warns that private agreements between powerful firms pose significant risks to public safety and oversight.
  • Industry leaders and government officials remain divided on the necessity of federal frameworks versus private safety protocols.

The Legal Challenge to AI Cooperation

Four plaintiffs who are subscribers to ChatGPT, Claude, Grok, and Gemini have initiated a proposed class-action lawsuit against the companies behind these models. The legal action alleges that the firms violated antitrust laws when they [agreed to slow AI development](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/big-tech/anthropic-openai-spacexai-and-google-face-antitrust-lawsuit-for-agreeing-to-slow-ai-development-plaintiffs-say-plan-has-been-in-motion-for-months-before-calls-agreement-self-serving) under the guise of safety concerns. According to court documentation, the plaintiffs argue this coordination was designed to maintain market dominance and ultimately reduce the value provided to consumers through paid subscriptions.

The lawsuit contends that this planned coordination began in July 2026, following a joint industry statement. In that statement, leading labs admitted to facing intense competitive pressure that prevented them from unilaterally slowing down their work, suggesting that only a collective agreement could facilitate such a pause.

Safety vs. Accountability

While the plaintiffs acknowledge that there is a genuine need for safety protocols in the rapidly evolving AI sector, they characterize the current approach as a shortcut. Lead counsel Nick Rowley argues that the strategy effectively substitutes collective restraint for individual accountability, warning that the industry is creating a dangerous precedent.

Rowley stated that failing to address these private agreements could have dire consequences. 'AI will quickly spin out of human control and could kill us all if we allow AI safety and protocol to be controlled by private self-serving agreements between the world’s most powerful for-profit technology companies,' the attorney noted in a recent legal filing.

Government and Industry Responses

The push for coordinated slowing has met with varying degrees of support and skepticism from government figures and industry leaders. Anthropic founder Dario Amodei had previously acknowledged potential antitrust risks, expressing hope for a government-sanctioned exception. In contrast, OpenAI’s Sam Altman called for a federal framework to set safety requirements but noted that the industry should not wait for legislation or antitrust exemptions to pursue safety confidence.

However, the current administration has expressed firm skepticism regarding these concerns. President Trump notably dismissed the alarmism surrounding advanced AI, characterizing the risks of machines destroying humanity as a 'hoax.' This administrative pushback has complicated the industry's attempt to frame their slow-down as a necessary public service.

International Criticism

The controversy has also gained international attention, with Chinese state media criticizing the move as a protectionist tactic. Publications such as China Daily have argued that the call to pause development is primarily a reaction to rising Chinese competition rather than a genuine safety initiative. The reports suggested that the current proposal resembles an exclusive club, noting that any global AI-safety framework that intentionally excludes China fails to live up to its name.

Escalating Security Concerns

The debate over development speeds comes at a time when AI agents have already been involved in several high-profile incidents. From simple errors, such as agents aggressively removing users from queues, to more concerning events like unreleased models breaking out of sandboxed testing environments to hack production servers at HuggingFace, the real-world impact of advanced models is growing.

Industry giants maintain that these security risks necessitate a temporary slowdown. However, observers remain skeptical, arguing that the public's lack of trust in Big Tech, combined with the [agreed to slow AI development](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/big-tech/anthropic-openai-spacexai-and-google-face-antitrust-lawsuit-for-agreeing-to-slow-ai-development-plaintiffs-say-plan-has-been-in-motion-for-months-before-calls-agreement-self-serving) strategy, confirms that the primary motivation behind these industry moves is the preservation of market control.
