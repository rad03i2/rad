OpenAI Calls for Global Standards to Guide AI Development

OpenAI is proposing a framework for shared global standards to manage the risks and opportunities presented by rapid advancements in artificial intelligence. The initiative focuses on maintaining human oversight during the development of increasingly autonomous systems.

Radwan AbdulhadiFounder and editor, Mikhbar
Published: September 22, 2026
Updated: September 22, 2026 · 3 min read

Key points

  • OpenAI outlines a strategic path toward global AI governance to address safety, reporting, and evaluation.
  • The proposal emphasizes the risks of unmanaged recursive self-improvement and the need for human control.
  • International standards are necessary to combat fragmentation, uneven technical capacity, and collective action challenges.
  • The organization advocates for the United States to lead international efforts in creating technical benchmarks for frontier AI.

Navigating the Future of AI

OpenAI has officially released a new roadmap detailing its vision for the next phase of artificial intelligence development. As outlined recently by Sam Altman, the organization remains focused on three core objectives: creating an automated AI researcher, translating scientific progress into economic growth, and providing individuals with personal AGI capabilities. These goals are central to the company’s mission of ensuring that artificial intelligence benefits all of humanity.

To achieve these objectives, OpenAI is prioritizing alignment research and the development of safety protocols. The company notes that AI is accelerating our own research and engineering, which has already facilitated significant breakthroughs, such as solving complex equations related to the Navier-Stokes Millennium Problem. These scientific successes serve as a foundation for broader applications in medicine and small business empowerment.

The Challenge of Recursive Self-Improvement

A primary focus of the new policy document is the concept of recursive self-improvement (RSI), where AI systems participate in the development of successive generations of technology. While this could lead to rapid progress, OpenAI warns that it requires careful management to prevent the loss of human oversight. The firm maintains that fully autonomous RSI is not current practice and should only proceed if it can be verified as safe.

The company stresses that if left unchecked, the transition to highly autonomous systems could result in diminished human control over research processes. Consequently, alignment research must be prioritized to keep pace with the increasing capabilities of AI models. By keeping alignment at the forefront, the organization believes that even automated systems can function as safety researchers, helping to secure infrastructure and build defenses against dangerous AI agents.

The Case for International Standardization

OpenAI argues that technical and safety standards are essential for the global advancement of frontier AI. The company suggests that current fragmented approaches—where different nations adopt varying reporting requirements and evaluation definitions—hinder the global community's ability to respond to cross-border risks. Establishing shared definitions of high-quality evidence would provide a more consistent framework for assessing catastrophic risks across the industry.

The proposal identifies three critical challenges that international standards aim to resolve: the issue of fragmentation, the need for collective action, and the problem of uneven capacity. Because AI deployment is global, OpenAI suggests that no single nation can effectively mitigate the risks of runaway AI development on its own. Instead, it advocates for a cooperative approach that includes public-private partnerships and the involvement of organizations like the Center for AI Standards and Innovation (CAISI).

Accountability and Global Leadership

OpenAI maintains that all laboratories engaged in frontier AI research must be held accountable for safe development practices, regardless of whether their models are open or closed. The push for standards is designed to avoid the concentration of power and to ensure that a broader range of stakeholders has influence over the direction of technological progress. This strategy aims to create visible principles that transcend the specific operational practices of any single firm.

Ultimately, OpenAI proposes that the United States should take a leadership role in fostering international collaboration. By working with global partners, the U.S. could help establish technical standards that apply to frontier AI development, including RSI processes. Through these coordinated efforts, the organization believes that the global community can ensure the safe, beneficial, and controlled evolution of next-generation artificial intelligence.
