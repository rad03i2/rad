Google Unveils New Googlebook Laptop Lineup

Google has officially introduced the Googlebook, a new premium line of laptops designed to bridge the gap between Android mobile devices and traditional desktop computing.

Radwan AbdulhadiFounder and editor, Mikhbar
Published: September 22, 2026
Updated: September 22, 2026 · 3 min read

Key points

  • The new Googlebook OS combines elements of Android and ChromeOS for a high-end experience.
  • Hardware partners at launch include Dell, Lenovo, HP, Asus, and Acer.
  • Devices feature high-end specifications including OLED screens and AI-focused Gemini integration.
  • Laptops start at $899, with most configurations priced at $1,299.

A New Chapter for Google Hardware

Following an initial tease [back in May](https://www.tomshardware.com/laptops/intel-confirms-googlebook-ai-laptop-partnership-opening-x86-possibilities-for-new-os-google-vp-says-devices-to-also-ship-with-qualcomm-and-mediatek-chips), Google has unveiled its comprehensive lineup of Googlebooks. These devices represent a significant shift in strategy, aiming to provide a premium, Mac-style experience for users deeply invested in the Android ecosystem. By combining the best aspects of ChromeOS and Android, the new Googlebook OS creates a cohesive environment that allows for seamless interaction between mobile and desktop hardware.

Hardware Partners and Premium Design

The inaugural wave of Googlebooks features hardware from five major manufacturers: Acer, Dell, Lenovo, Asus, and HP. These companies are utilizing premium materials such as aluminum, magnesium, and carbon fiber to position the laptops against competitive devices like the MacBook Air and high-end Windows machines. The displays are largely defined by high-resolution panels, including several 2880 x 1800 OLED options, and the majority of the lineup features haptic touchpads.

Internally, the machines are equipped with either Intel Core Ultra or Qualcomm silicon. Google has set a high performance floor for the series, requiring a minimum of 16GB of RAM and 256GB of storage across all models. An aesthetic highlight of the new laptops is the 'Glowbar' located behind the screen—a series of RGB lights that provide visual feedback for startup, battery status, and Gemini-related interactions.

Software Integration and Ecosystem

The Googlebook OS is designed for deep integration with Android phones. Once a user logs in, the operating system syncs files, photos, apps, and credentials, ensuring a unified experience across devices. While much of the platform relies on browser-based workflows through Chrome, it includes a dedicated file manager with AI-driven descriptions. According to [Google](https://www.tomshardware.com/tag/google), approximately 70% of time on a laptop is spent in a web browser, which remains a core pillar of the OS design.

Applications are sourced from the Play Store, with various titles—including Adobe Photoshop, Lightroom, CapCut, and Netflix—offering specialized desktop modes. For connectivity, the 'Continue On' feature mimics macOS and iOS continuity, allowing users to transition tasks between mobile and laptop. Additionally, the 'Cast My Apps' functionality enables users to control their phone directly from the laptop screen.

AI Capabilities and Availability

Gemini AI is baked directly into the operating system, with the 'Magic Pointer' feature serving as a centerpiece for user interaction. By wiggling the cursor, the tool becomes a Gemini-focused interface, allowing users to click on screen elements for contextual suggestions. Beyond standard productivity, the platform includes 'Antigravity,' a development toolset for creating agentic workflows and software directly on the device, including a Linux terminal environment.

Pricing for the new lineup is firmly in the premium category, with the Acer Chromebook 14 starting at $899 as a limited-time offer, while the Dell XPS and other models start at $1,199 and $1,299, respectively. Prospective buyers can pre-order the devices starting today, with retail availability scheduled for October 4 in the U.S. and October 5 across several international markets including Canada, the U.K., and Germany. For more information, read the full report at [Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/laptops/hands-on-with-googlebooks-five-models-the-new-googlebook-os-and-a-mac-style-experience-for-android-users-at-premium-prices).
