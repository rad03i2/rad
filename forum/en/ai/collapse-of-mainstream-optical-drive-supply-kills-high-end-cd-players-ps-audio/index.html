Optical Drive Shortage Halts High-End CD Player Production by PS Audio and Naim

The shortage of optical drives, critical components for high-end CD players, has led PS Audio and Naim to cease production, with costs doubling as manufacturers exit the market.

Radwan AbdulhadiFounder and editor, RDWAN Tech
Published: September 21, 2026
Updated: September 21, 2026 · 3 min read

Key points

  • PS Audio and Naim halt production of premium CD players due to optical drive supply collapse
  • Component costs for optical drives have doubled as manufacturers discontinue production
  • Audiophiles face limited options as replacement parts become unavailable for existing devices
  • CD and vinyl sales continue growing despite supply chain challenges for physical media hardware

Industry Crisis in Hi-Fi Audio Hardware

High-end audio manufacturers PS Audio and Naim have ceased production of two premium CD player models following a critical shortage of optical drives, according to [Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/peripherals/audiophiles-in-trouble-as-manufacturers-cease-optical-drive-production-hi-fi-cd-players-require-extensive-rework-to-use-components-from-other-companies). The shortage stems from major suppliers discontinuing optical drive production for mainstream computers, creating a ripple effect across audiophile-grade hardware manufacturing.

The crisis highlights a paradox in the audio industry: while physical media like CDs and vinyl have experienced unprecedented sales growth in 2026, the hardware ecosystem supporting these formats is rapidly deteriorating. Existing owners of affected devices can still access repair services through remaining component inventories, but new purchases of these models are no longer possible.

Supply Chain Collapse Triggers Production Halts

PS Audio's problems began in June 2024 when D&M Holdings announced the discontinuation of its SACD/CD mechanism production. The company's final order deadline forced PS Audio to double component costs while competitors scrambled to stockpile inventory. As [PS Audio CEO Paul McGowan explained](https://www.psaudio.com/products/pmg-signature-sacd-transport), 'The drives appropriate for a PMG are no longer available,' citing the company's inability to manufacture optical drives in-house due to its focus on electronics and software.

Naim's Uniti Star Streaming Amplifier faces similar challenges, with the company exploring alternative suppliers before their chosen provider also ceased production. Audiophile forums reveal that optical drives often require extensive mechanical and software modifications when swapped, making alternative sourcing technically complex and economically unfeasible for mass production.

Technical Barriers to Component Replacement

Industry experts note that optical drives for high-end audio equipment are typically integrated as complete assemblies rather than modular components. This design requires new control codes and mechanical adjustments when substituting different models, creating significant engineering hurdles for manufacturers attempting to pivot to alternative suppliers.

The situation reflects broader supply chain vulnerabilities, where specialized components for niche markets become unavailable when mainstream demand shifts. While standard CD players remain purchasable, audiophile-grade transports requiring SACD compatibility face particular scarcity due to their limited production volumes and complex encryption requirements.

Market Dynamics Defy Industry Expectations

Contrary to expectations, physical media sales have surged in 2026, with CD sales increasing 59% and vinyl 18% in the first half of the year according to [industry reports](https://www.tomshardware.com/service-providers/streaming/sales-of-cd-and-vinyl-music-sees-strong-resurgence-amid-physical-media-backlash-us-sales-of-retro-media-were-up-59-percent-and-18-percent-respectively-in-h1-2026). This resurgence has intensified pressure on manufacturers struggling to maintain production capabilities.

Audio enthusiasts cite multiple factors driving physical media adoption, including superior sound quality, ownership rights, and nostalgia for tangible formats. However, the hardware supporting these preferences is becoming increasingly scarce, creating a growing divide between consumer demand and manufacturing capability.

Future Implications for Audiophile Community

The optical drive shortage underscores the fragility of specialized hardware ecosystems. While existing PS Audio and Naim owners can access repair services through remaining component inventories, potential buyers face limited options. Industry analysts suggest this may accelerate interest in alternative formats like high-resolution digital files or hybrid solutions combining physical and digital media.

The situation also raises questions about the sustainability of premium audio hardware production in an era of declining mainstream demand for optical media. Manufacturers may need to explore partnerships with component suppliers or develop new technologies to ensure long-term viability of their product lines.
