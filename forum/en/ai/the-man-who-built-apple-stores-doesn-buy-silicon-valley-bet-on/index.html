Apple Store Architect Skeptical of AI-Driven Shopping Future

Ron Johnson believes Silicon Valley is overestimating the role of automation in consumer behavior, asserting that personal, tactile experiences remain irreplaceable.

Radwan AbdulhadiFounder and editor, Mikhbar
Published: September 22, 2026
Updated: September 22, 2026 · 3 min read

Key points

  • Ron Johnson argues that high-stakes purchases like laptops require physical interaction that AI cannot replicate.
  • Major tech players are aggressively pursuing 'agentic commerce' to automate the end-to-end shopping journey.
  • The former Apple executive emphasizes that human intuition and in-store staff remain the 'secret sauce' of successful retail.
  • Johnson advocates for an 'AI optimist' view where technology assists research without replacing the final human decision.

The Rise of Agentic Commerce

While Silicon Valley heavyweights are pouring billions into the development of autonomous shopping tools, Ron Johnson, the architect behind the success of [Apple](https://techcrunch.com/tag/apple/) retail, remains unconvinced that machines will displace the human element of commerce. As the industry pushes toward [agentic commerce](https://techcrunch.com/tag/agentic-commerce/), which aims to automate the entire path from product discovery to final purchase, Johnson suggests that these ambitions ignore the fundamental psychological drivers of consumer behavior.

Companies like Google are spearheading these efforts with the [Universal Commerce Protocol](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/11/google-announces-a-new-protocol-to-facilitate-commerce-using-ai-agents/), a technical standard intended to streamline how AI agents manage consumer transactions. Simultaneously, OpenAI has been [turning ChatGPT into a shopping destination](https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/28/openai-upgrades-chatgpt-search-with-shopping-features/) to allow users to research and finalize purchases without navigating away from the conversational interface.

The Irreplaceable Nature of Physical Retail

Johnson, who detailed his philosophy in his new book [Shop Different: How Retail Revealed Apple’s Genius](https://www.harpercollins.com/products/shop-different-ron-johnsonzander-nethercutt?variant=44771032498210), posits that the complexity of high-value purchases prevents total automation. When asked if a consumer would realistically permit an AI agent to select and purchase a high-end laptop without seeing the device in person, Johnson offered a firm rejection: 'Honestly, nobody’s going to do that.'

For Johnson, the physical experience is a prerequisite for a significant investment. Consumers wish to hold a product, test its weight, and verify its display quality—sensory inputs that current [AI](https://techcrunch.com/category/artificial-intelligence/) models cannot facilitate. Instead of replacing the store, he argues that artificial intelligence will likely serve as a sophisticated research tool, ensuring customers arrive at retail locations with a higher degree of product knowledge than ever before.

Lessons from a Retail Career

Johnson’s skepticism is rooted in decades of operational experience. After joining Apple in 2000, he helped design a retail network centered on the belief that human interaction, rather than pure transactionality, defined the brand. He noted that while competitors frequently mimicked the visual aesthetics of the Apple Store—including open layouts and glass-heavy designs—they failed to replicate the unique sales culture. According to Johnson, the 'secret sauce' was always the employees, who were incentivized to solve customer problems rather than work on commission.

This perspective was further tempered by his later career experiences. After he [took over](https://fortune.com/2011/06/14/the-man-who-built-the-apple-stores-leaves-for-j-c-penney/) as CEO of J.C. Penney in 2011, he was eventually [ousted](https://www.theguardian.com/business/2013/apr/08/jc-penney-ron-johnson-mike-ullman) after a rapid attempt at transformation failed to resonate with the existing customer base. He admits that he incorrectly applied a 'startup mentality' to a company requiring a different type of turnaround strategy.

An Optimist’s Outlook on Technology

Despite his critical stance on the automation of shopping, Johnson does not harbor an anti-technology bias. He [returned to the startup world](https://techcrunch.com/2019/08/27/ron-johnsons-e-commerce-startup-enjoy-raises-150m-expands-in-u-k/) to found Enjoy Technology, an entity that sought to combine high-touch service with tech products. Although that venture eventually [filed for bankruptcy in 2022](https://www.cnbc.com/2022/06/30/ex-apple-exec-ron-johnsons-enjoy-technology-files-for-bankruptcy.html), Johnson maintains a firm belief in the potential of AI to improve consumer experiences.

Reflecting on the legacy of Steve Jobs, Johnson suggests that while the late Apple co-founder would have embraced AI, he would have treated it as a complement to human judgment rather than a substitute. 'There’s no substitute for human intuition,' Johnson concluded, emphasizing that innovation flourishes when intelligent people convene to debate, analyze, and synthesize new information, a process that continues to form the backbone of his retail philosophy.
