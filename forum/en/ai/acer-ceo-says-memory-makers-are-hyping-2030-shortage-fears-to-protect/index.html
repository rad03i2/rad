Acer CEO Challenges 2030 Memory Shortage Predictions

Acer CEO Jason Chen disputes industry claims regarding long-term component scarcity, suggesting that market competition will eventually drive down costs despite current manufacturer forecasts.

Radwan AbdulhadiFounder and editor, RDWAN Tech
Published: September 21, 2026
Updated: September 21, 2026 · 3 min read

Key points

  • Acer expects PC prices to plateau in early 2027 before declining.
  • Jason Chen claims memory suppliers are exaggerating shortage fears to protect profit margins.
  • The emergence of Chinese memory capacity is expected to disrupt current pricing trends.
  • While some high-end components remain in short supply, general memory and SSD availability is improving.

Market Forecasts and Price Trends

Acer chairperson and CEO Jason Chen recently shared his outlook on the PC component market, offering a perspective that stands in contrast to the warnings issued by several major memory manufacturers. While the industry has seen significant volatility, Chen anticipates that the upward trajectory of PC prices will likely plateau by the first half of 2027. Following this stabilization period, he projects a meaningful decline in prices toward the latter part of the year, providing a potential reprieve after years of AI-driven cost hikes. This perspective comes as many in the industry have noted how [memory prices climb 500%] over the past year, reflecting the intense pressure on supply chains.

The Debate Over Long-Term Shortages

The conflict in outlooks centers on how long the current supply constraints will persist. Companies like SK hynix have cautioned that market conditions could remain difficult well into 2030, while other industry leaders have suggested that the DRAM shortage may impact the sector for the next decade. These forecasts are generally based on the assumption that strong demand for high-bandwidth memory (HBM) from AI hyperscalers will continue to exhaust existing production capacity, with new fabrication plants not expected to reach significant output until the 2030s. However, Chen remains skeptical of these long-term timelines.

According to the Acer executive, major memory suppliers are motivated by a desire to protect their gross margins for as long as possible. Chen suggested that the rhetoric regarding a 2030 supply crisis is a strategic narrative designed to influence market expectations. He argued that there is already sufficient supply of memory and SSD components, and that the narrative of a prolonged, market-wide shortage is being used to keep pricing elevated. Although the market has faced intense pressure, industry observers have noted that [increases have seemingly cooled] as consumers have hit an affordability limit.

The Role of New Market Players

A key factor in Chen's optimistic outlook is the rise of alternative suppliers. He pointed out that Chinese memory manufacturers are beginning to churn out more affordable, viable alternatives to the industry incumbents. This increased capacity is expected to disrupt the current market pricing power held by larger suppliers. The impact of this shift is already visible, as Acer, HP, and Asus have begun integrating CXMT memory chips into their product lineups. Similarly, some Lenovo models available in the German market have been confirmed to feature YMTC SSDs, signaling a diversification of the component supply chain that could weaken the influence of established players.

Current Constraints and Ongoing Costs

While Chen is optimistic about the long-term, he acknowledged that certain localized shortages still persist. High-end components, specifically LPDDR5X-9600 memory and certain niche Nvidia chips like the N1 and N1X, continue to face supply challenges. Additionally, there is a persistent trend of manufacturers attempting to raise prices on secondary components. Chen described receiving frequent requests for price increases on items such as PCBs and the fiberglass cloth used in motherboards, which he characterized as baffling. Despite these pressures, the Acer leader believes the overall environment is trending toward more reasonable consumer costs, provided that the current reliance on AI-driven supply narratives does not continue to artificially inflate market pricing.
